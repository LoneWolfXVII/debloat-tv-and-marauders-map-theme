# Clean, Reskin and Bulletproof Your Micromax TV

You can debloat a Micromax smart TV and give it a custom character-themed home screen without rooting it. Almost every step can be undone, provided you do three things first. **Identify which of Micromax's four software generations you own.** **Prove that network ADB comes back after a cold power-cycle.** **Keep the stock launcher installed until your own launcher has held Home through a reboot.** CleanYourTV gives you the safest starting point. It only ever runs `pm disable-user --user 0` and never uninstalls. It snapshots the TV's state before changing anything, refuses to touch a hard-coded list of core packages, and rolls Home back automatically if the takeover fails ([CleanYourTV](https://github.com/Gathin23/cleanyourtv)). The caveat is that **CleanYourTV has no tested Micromax list**: a grep of the whole repo finds zero mentions of "micromax", and no Micromax debloat or brick reports turned up anywhere online. You will be the first person documenting this hardware. For the custom UI, the safest plan keeps two risks apart. Build the character lineup and the mood-based "random watch" screen as an ordinary sideloaded Kotlin/Compose for TV app first, then promote it to Home only once it works. That way your UI experiments can never strand the TV on a black screen. The recovery plan has five steps, from least to most drastic: ADB re-enable, Settings, safe mode, recovery-mode factory reset, firmware reflash. **A factory reset undoes every user-0 debloat change**, because those changes live in /data ([UAD-ng FAQ](https://github.com/Universal-Debloater-Alliance/universal-android-debloater-next-generation/wiki/FAQ)). A reflash, the one truly dangerous step, should almost never be needed.

## Your Micromax generation decides which plan works

Micromax has shipped at least four distinct software stacks in India, and they behave very differently.

| Generation | OS | Store | Debloat via ADB | CleanYourTV launcher | Custom Compose launcher |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2015 4K sets (42C0050UHD etc.) | AOSP Android 4.2 / 4.4 | none / Play (unverified) | Effectively no (`disable-user` needs Android 6+ without root) | No | No (minSdk 23) |
| 2018 "Canvas 3" (32/40/50) | AOSP Android 7.0 | Aptoide | Yes | **No**: needs SDK 26 | **Yes** (SDK 24 ≥ 23) |
| 2018 49/55-inch 4K | Google-certified Android TV | Play | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| 2019 32/40/43-inch (32CAM6SHD, 40CAM6SFHD) | Google-certified Android TV | Play + Chromecast | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| "Custom Android" / "Android Based" budget sets (32TA6445HD, 40V1107HD) | Unknown, probably AOSP | Vendor | Probably | Check SDK first | Probably |

The 2015 sets ran Jelly Bean and KitKat ([Ubergizmo](https://www.ubergizmo.com/2015/03/micromax-unveils-android-powered-4k-tvs-in-india-starting-from-640/amp/)). The **Canvas 3 range runs Android 7.0 Nougat with the Aptoide store, 1 GB RAM and 5.5 GB storage** ([FoneArena](https://www.fonearena.com/blog/263265/micromax-canvas-3-smart-tv-price-india.html); [The Mobile Indian](https://themobileindian.com/news/micromax-announces-canvas-3-smart-tv-with-android-70-aptoide-store-23692)). Google-certified 4K models arrived in 2018 ([ElectronicsForU](https://ace.electronicsforu.com/?p=68943)). The July 2019 32/40/43-inch line has Play Store, Chromecast and Assistant, with 1.5–2.5 GB RAM ([Digit](https://www.digit.in/news/tvs/micromax-launches-32-inch-40-inch-and-43-inch-android-smart-tvs-starting-at-rs-13999-49074.html)). Budget listings that say only "Custom Android" exist too ([Smartprix](https://smartprix.com/tvs/micromax-32ta6445hd-32-inch-hd-ready-smart-ppd14458kbxy)). Most of the decision follows from two numbers. CleanYourTV's install script refuses any TV where `ro.build.version.sdk` is below 26 (`INSTALL_FAILED_OLDER_SDK`). Current AndroidX/Compose requires minSdk 23 ([build-and-install.sh](https://github.com/Gathin23/cleanyourtv/blob/main/launcher/build-and-install.sh); [Zendesk SDK notice](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/10934369609370-Announcing-a-new-minimum-Android-version-requirement-for-the-Zendesk-messaging-SDK)). So **on a Canvas 3, the only way to get a custom home screen is a launcher you build yourself**. CleanYourTV's own launcher cannot install there. On a KitKat set, a non-root debloat is not realistic, because disabling packages without root needs Android 6 or later ([UAD-ng FAQ](https://github.com/Universal-Debloater-Alliance/universal-android-debloater-next-generation/wiki/FAQ)).

Identify the set in two minutes, without any tools. If the TV has the Google Play Store and built-in Chromecast, it is certified Android TV and probably uses `com.google.android.tvlauncher`. If it has Aptoide or a vendor store, it is an AOSP build with an OEM launcher whose package name nobody has published. Once ADB connects, `getprop` confirms the answer. A `google/` fingerprint means a certified build. The `ro.board.platform` value tells you the SoC family. `pm list packages` shows the ODM stack: `com.mstar.*`, `com.cvte.*`, `com.mediatek.*` or `com.realtek.*` ([Android Developers: adb](https://developer.android.com/tools/adb)). Public specs almost never name the SoC. The only clues are "MediaTek processors" for the 2019 certified line ([Beebom](https://beebom.com/micromax-android-tv-launched-india)), a Mali-470 MP3 GPU on 32CAM6SHD ([Gizbot](https://www.gizbot.com/smart-tv/micromax-led-smart-android-tv-32cam6shd-81cm-32-inch-hd-ready-153/)), and an MStar-style board code, 3MSV56LT5AP.01, tied to one Micromax 32-inch ([dipelectronicslab](https://www.dipelectronicslab.com/micromax-3msv56lt5ap-01-software/)). The safe assumption is a MediaTek/MStar or CVTE ODM board. That matters, because CleanYourTV's worst incidents cluster on exactly those boards.

## CleanYourTV handles the debloat, but Micromax is uncharted

CleanYourTV is an MIT-licensed kit created on 2026-10-17. It had **51K stars, 6.4K forks and tested lists for 427 models from 111 brands** as of late October ([CleanYourTV README](https://github.com/Gathin23/cleanyourtv#the-story)). It is built around a coding agent. You clone the repo and hand `PROMPT.md` to Claude Code, Codex or Gemini CLI. The agent then drives network ADB using the repo's own scripts, `tools/disable.sh` and `launcher/build-and-install.sh`, "where the backup, the Home guard and the restore record live" ([README Quick start](https://github.com/Gathin23/cleanyourtv#quick-start)). Clone the repo; don't just paste the prompt. PCWorld's warning against blindly running AI-generated TV debloat commands applies directly here ([PCWorld](https://www.pcworld.com/article/3266473/please-dont-use-ai-hacks-to-debloat-your-tv.html)). CleanYourTV's guards exist precisely to stop an agent improvising on an unknown TV, so keep the agent on those scripts.

On the Mac you need Python 3, **JDK 17–22 (JDK 23+ breaks Gradle 8.9)**, and Android SDK platform 34 with build-tools 34 (`tools/docker-build.sh` is an alternative) ([README](https://github.com/Gathin23/cleanyourtv#doing-it-by-hand)). On the TV, open Settings → Device Preferences → About, press Build seven times, then enable USB debugging and "Network debugging" if the toggle exists ([README](https://github.com/Gathin23/cleanyourtv#quick-start)). The Android 11+ pairing-code "Wireless debugging" flow (`adb pair`) is almost certainly absent, because no Micromax TV on Android 11 or later turned up in research ([Android Developers: adb](https://developer.android.com/tools/adb)). Your route is plain `adb connect <TV-IP>:5555`, approved by an RSA prompt on screen. If macOS's Local Network privacy blocks the `adb` binary with "No route to host", CleanYourTV falls back to a pure-Python ADB client (`tools/tv.py`) and pushes files in md5-verified base64 chunks ([PROMPT.md](https://github.com/Gathin23/cleanyourtv/blob/main/PROMPT.md)). That is a common snag on recent macOS.

The debloat itself has several safety features. Unclear OEM packages are inspected with `dumpsys package` and never launched. Packages are disabled one `pm` call at a time, in small batches with screenshots between them, and the result is checked with `pm list packages -d` rather than exit codes. The agent waits about 15 seconds before rebooting because Android writes package state about 10 seconds after a change ([PROMPT.md](https://github.com/Gathin23/cleanyourtv/blob/main/PROMPT.md); [disable.sh](https://github.com/Gathin23/cleanyourtv/blob/main/tools/disable.sh)). A hard-coded `CORE_RE` guard refuses any list that contains `android`, SystemUI, Settings, `com.android.tv.settings`, the package installer, the permission controller, `com.android.providers.*`, `com.android.sdksandbox`, `com.android.location.fused`, or the MediaTek middleware `com.mediatek.(tis|tv.service|tv.agent)` ([disable.sh L59-73](https://github.com/Gathin23/cleanyourtv/blob/main/tools/disable.sh)). Each of those entries exists because a real TV broke without it. **A Mi TV Stick never booted again (#884), a TCL Google TV stuck at its logo after `sdksandbox` was disabled (#2557), and a TCL boot-looped until `location.fused` was protected (#2803)** ([Issue #2557](https://github.com/Gathin23/cleanyourtv/issues/2557); [Issue #2803](https://github.com/Gathin23/cleanyourtv/issues/2803)). The cases closest to a Micromax are the worrying ones. A CVTE NOVA set was soft-bricked after its CVTE launcher was disabled and the power then failed (#2516). An Android 4.4 Realtek set stuck on its boot screen with ADB unreachable after 17 disables (#795). On a OnePlus TV, removing OEM services broke the HDMI handshake (#321) ([Issue #2516](https://github.com/Gathin23/cleanyourtv/issues/2516); [Issue #795](https://github.com/Gathin23/cleanyourtv/issues/795); [Issue #321](https://github.com/Gathin23/cleanyourtv/issues/321)).

That evidence leads to a firm rule for a Micromax with no tested list. **Disable only user-facing bloat**: Aptoide once you have another store, Facebook/Twitter preloads, partner content apps and recommendation/ad clients. **Never touch anything named `mstar`, `cvte`, `mediatek`, `realtek` or `tv`.** On MStar AOSP sets the "tvplayer" app often *is* the HDMI/antenna source, and removing it kills inputs. Leave the remote/Bluetooth pairing service, the on-screen keyboard, WebView, Play Store and Play Services alone, along with the updater, which on some MStar/CVTE builds also backs the "Upgrade from USB" entry you might need for recovery. Never open a factory app such as `com.cvte.tv.fac.menu`. On CVTE boards, opening one flips a factory-mode flag that kills the Input key and HDMI-CEC ([PROMPT.md](https://github.com/Gathin23/cleanyourtv/blob/main/PROMPT.md)). If `pm list packages` shows any `com.cvte.*`, CleanYourTV's own rule applies: **keep the stock `com.cvte.tv.launcher.*` enabled**, set Home with `set-home-activity`, and reboot before doing anything else. A JVC CVTE set hung at its boot animation with ADB gone when this rule was ignored (#741) ([PROMPT.md](https://github.com/Gathin23/cleanyourtv/blob/main/PROMPT.md)). Because your stock launcher won't match the four package names the install script knows, it will stop and ask for `STOCK_LAUNCHER=<pkg>` or `none`. On an AOSP Micromax, choose `none` (hybrid mode, stock launcher left enabled) until you trust the board ([build-and-install.sh](https://github.com/Gathin23/cleanyourtv/blob/main/launcher/build-and-install.sh); [themes/README.md](https://github.com/Gathin23/cleanyourtv/blob/main/themes/README.md)).

The Home takeover is where CleanYourTV's design pays off. It installs with `pm install -r` as a standalone call, so a failed install can't be followed by disabling the stock launcher. It requires `am start` of the new launcher to return `Status: ok` before touching the old one. It runs `set-home-activity` **last**, because "Android drops the preference whenever the set of HOME apps changes." It then re-queries Home and rolls back automatically on a mismatch, writing the rescue commands to `~/cleanyourtv-handover-<ip>.txt` ([build-and-install.sh L71-200](https://github.com/Gathin23/cleanyourtv/blob/main/launcher/build-and-install.sh)). On a certified Micromax, `tvlauncher` generally honours this. The Google TV problems (launcherx silently retaking Home, setupwraith crash-looping system_server, broken voice results and casting) belong to the newer Google TV stack, which no Micromax set ships ([TROUBLESHOOTING.md](https://github.com/Gathin23/cleanyourtv/blob/main/docs/TROUBLESHOOTING.md)). Still, keep Chromecast in mind. One Chromecast with Google TV owner was warned that disabling the stock launcher loses casting, and community advice there favoured an accessibility-based Home-button override over disabling ([r/AndroidTV](https://redlib.ssps.io/r/AndroidTV/comments/1k9gfpx/release_android_tv_debloat_toolkit_clean_safe/mpsfflp/?context=3)). Treat casting as a feature to test after the swap. When you finish, close the door you opened. **Port 5555 is plaintext, and with `ro.adb.secure=0` any device on your LAN gets a shell.** CleanYourTV's last step turns debugging off, revokes authorisations, and confirms with `tools/adb-closed.py` that the port refuses connections ([README FAQ](https://github.com/Gathin23/cleanyourtv#questions-people-ask)).

## Build the character UI as an app before it becomes Home

The cleanest architecture for your goals decouples UI risk from Home risk. Build the character lineup and the mood screen as a normal sideloaded TV app with a `LEANBACK_LAUNCHER` intent filter. Launch it from CleanYourTV's dock (certified sets) or from the stock launcher (Canvas 3). Iterate on it with `adb install -r` as often as you like; a crash can only ever return you to a working home screen. Once it is stable, add `HOME` and `DEFAULT` to the same intent filter and run the same guarded handover flow. On an SDK-26+ set you can stop earlier. CleanYourTV already provides the clean dock (4–7 tiles), an app grid that slides up on Down, an Inputs pill backed by the TV Input Framework, a clock, and Wi-Fi-pushed themes with `wallpapers/` collections, `cinematic_home` artwork and `dock_apps` favourites ([README "What you get"](https://github.com/Gathin23/cleanyourtv#what-you-get); [themes/README.md](https://github.com/Gathin23/cleanyourtv/blob/main/themes/README.md)). That covers your "clean dock + app grid" requirement without code. Only the character and mood features need new work. Its `wallpaper_moods` setting groups wallpapers by time of day, not by how you feel, so the mood screen is genuinely new ([themes/README.md](https://github.com/Gathin23/cleanyourtv/blob/main/themes/README.md)).

There is a real choice between forking CleanYourTV's launcher and writing your own, and the honest case for forking is strong. `com.gathin.tvhome` is about **572 KB of plain Java with no network permission and no background services, built for 1 GB-RAM TVs**. It already encodes hard-won TV edge cases: an explicitly opaque theme so the HDMI picture doesn't bleed through, `<queries>` for `LEANBACK_LAUNCHER` so Android 11+ doesn't hide every app, a `VIEW_INPUTS` handler, a long-press-Home fix, and accessibility services for remapping remote keys ([README "The launcher"](https://github.com/Gathin23/cleanyourtv#the-launcher); [AndroidManifest.xml](https://github.com/Gathin23/cleanyourtv/blob/main/launcher/app/src/main/AndroidManifest.xml); [PROMPT.md](https://github.com/Gathin23/cleanyourtv/blob/main/PROMPT.md)). Community forks such as Weeb glass home (anime characters, with the focus colour taken from each picture), Home Deck (fan-art favourites) and Kids World (mascots) show the pattern working ([SHOWCASE.md](https://github.com/Gathin23/cleanyourtv/blob/main/SHOWCASE.md)). The case against forking, for you, is ergonomics. The UI is RecyclerView plus imperative Java animation, about as far from React + GSAP as Android gets, and it cannot run on a Canvas 3 at all. **Kotlin + Compose for TV is the better fit for a frontend developer**. Google calls Compose "the recommended way to build apps for Android TV OS", and `androidx.tv:tv-material` 1.1.0 is stable as of May 2026 ([Set up a TV project](https://developer.android.com/training/tv/get-started/create); [androidx.tv release notes](https://developer.android.com/jetpack/androidx/releases/tv)). Focus state drives the UI the way React state does. `animateFloatAsState`, `Crossfade` and `AnimatedContent` take the place of GSAP tweens. The platform handles D-pad traversal, so you don't build a spatial-navigation engine. A WebView shell would reuse your HTML/CSS/GSAP skills, but budget AOSP TVs often carry an old, non-updatable System WebView. Each WebView costs tens of MB of RAM on a 1–1.5 GB device, and you would still need native code for app listing and launching. Treat it as a fallback, and check the TV's WebView first with `adb shell dumpsys webviewupdate`. If you write your own launcher, copy CleanYourTV's manifest choices and its `build-and-install.sh` flow (with your package substituted), and use FLauncher's verified manifest as a second template. That manifest uses `singleTask` so Home presses don't stack instances, and a long `configChanges` list so HDMI or density changes don't recreate the activity ([FLauncher AndroidManifest.xml](https://gitlab.com/flauncher/flauncher/-/raw/master/android/app/src/main/AndroidManifest.xml)).

The "My Favourites" lineup maps cleanly onto Compose. Use a `LazyRow` of tv-material `Surface` items; tv-material gives you Border/Glow/Scale focus indications for free. Animate a saturation `ColorMatrix` from 0 to 1 and scale inside `graphicsLayer {}` so recomposition is skipped. Drive a full-screen `Crossfade` background from the focused index. Google removed tv-material's `ImmersiveList` (background follows focus) in 1.0.0-beta01 and now points to [a sample](https://cs.android.com/androidx/platform/frameworks/support/+/androidx-main:tv/samples/src/main/java/androidx/tv/samples/ImmersiveListSamples.kt;drc=5920fece16ad6723107098f24a492a25937cd51a) showing how to build it yourself. That sample is your per-character background template ([androidx.tv release notes](https://developer.android.com/jetpack/androidx/releases/tv)). Three platform facts constrain the visuals. **Glow indication does nothing below API 28**, so Canvas 3 users see no glow. Scaled text jitters, so scale the character image and keep labels static. The first item in a scrolling container doesn't take focus by default, so set initial focus with a `FocusRequester` ([androidx.tv release notes](https://developer.android.com/jetpack/androidx/releases/tv)). Memory is the binding limit on these TVs. Decoding at display size turns a 12 MB bitmap into 0.75 MB ([Loading large bitmaps efficiently](https://developer.android.com/topic/performance/graphics/load-bitmap)). Pre-render backgrounds at the panel's real resolution (check `adb shell wm size`; 720p is common), keep only the current and next scene decoded, and debounce rapid scrolling by about 150 ms. If animated saturation stutters on the Mali GPU, crossfade between pre-baked grey and colour PNGs instead. `Modifier.blur` needs API 31 and won't render on these TVs, and video or Lottie-heavy backgrounds will drop frames. These limits come from engineering judgement rather than published Mali benchmarks, so profile on the TV with "Profile HWUI rendering" and `dumpsys gfxinfo`, using an R8 release build.

### Deep links work, but every format must be tested on your TV

The mood screen is a JSON catalogue of `{title, mood[], app, uri, fallback}` entries. You pick a random entry for the chosen mood and fire an explicit `VIEW` intent pinned to the app's package, so the browser can't intercept `https://` links. On `ActivityNotFoundException` or a non-matching link, fall back to `getLeanbackLaunchIntentForPackage()`. **No streaming service publishes Android TV deep-link specs**, so everything below is community-reported ([HA deep-linking guide](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/android-tv-remote-app-links-deep-linking-guide/567921)).

| Service | TV package | Reported URI | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Netflix | `com.netflix.ninja` | `https://www.netflix.com/title/<id>` (details); `/watch/<id>` + extra `source=30` (play) | Community, old app version ([SO 35556634](https://kiwix.lema.org/stackoverflow.com_en_all_2023-11/questions/35556634/movie-deeplink-for-netflix-android-tv-app-com-netflix-ninja)) |
| YouTube | `com.google.android.youtube.tv` (unverified) | `vnd.youtube:<id>`, `https://www.youtube.com/...` | Community ([HA guide](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/android-tv-remote-app-links-deep-linking-guide/567921)) |
| Prime Video | `com.amazon.amazonvideo.livingroom` | `https://app.primevideo.com/...` | Package solid ([APKMirror](https://apkmirror.com/apk/amazon-mobile-llc/prime-video-android-tv-android-tv/prime-video-android-tv-6-16-5v14-1-0-772-armv7a-release/prime-video-android-tv-6-16-5v14-1-0-772-armv7a-android-apk-download)); URI shape unverified |
| JioHotstar | `in.startv.hotstar.dplus.tv` (pre-merger ID) | none documented | Package from APKMirror ([APKMirror](https://apkmirror.com/apk/the-walt-disney-company-southeast-asia-pte-ltd/disney-hotstar-android-tv/disney-hotstar-android-tv-25-02-24-7-release/disney-hotstar-android-tv-25-02-24-7-2-android-apk-download)); deep links reported failing ([Codidact](https://software.codidact.com/posts/287382)) |

The weak spots are Prime and JioHotstar. Phone versions of these apps often accept deep links that the TV builds ignore ([ADBTuner thread](https://community.getchannels.com/t/adbtuner-a-channel-tuning-application-for-networked-google-tv-android-tv-devices/36822?page=48)), and the JioCinema–Hotstar merger has probably changed the TV package. Read the real intent filters off your own TV with `adb shell dumpsys package <pkg>` (the Activity Resolver Table). Test each URI with `adb shell am start -W -a android.intent.action.VIEW -d "<uri>" -p <pkg>`, and watch `adb logcat -s ActivityTaskManager:I` to see what actually resolved ([HA guide](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/android-tv-remote-app-links-deep-linking-guide/567921)). Design the mood screen to "open the right app" by default and treat landing on a specific title as a bonus. For JioHotstar, plan on the app-launch fallback. Ship the catalogue in `assets/` and read an override from `/sdcard/Download/catalogue.json`, so you can curate moods by `adb push` without rebuilding. Develop against an arm64 Android TV emulator on Apple Silicon for logic only; it tells you nothing about Mali performance ([Set up a TV project](https://developer.android.com/training/tv/get-started/create)). Fan art of real characters is fine on your own TV, but keep it out of any public repo or shared APK.

## The recovery ladder ends at a factory reset, not a reflash

The whole safety case rests on one fact that is easy to miss: **on a TV, wireless ADB is your only way in.** TV USB-A ports are host-only and generally cannot act as an ADB device port. `adb tcpip 5555` is lost on reboot unless you set `persist.adb.tcp.port`, which needs root ([Android Developers: adb](https://developer.android.com/tools/adb); [knightli](https://knightli.com/en/2020/12/28/adb-tcpip-persistent/)). The debugging toggle and your Mac's authorised RSA key survive reboots, but whether port 5555 listens after a cold boot depends on the firmware. So **before you disable a single package, pull the AC plug, wait, power on, and run `adb connect` again.** If it reconnects without touching the remote, every package mistake stays fixable from the Mac. If it doesn't, the only non-root workaround is toggling USB debugging off and on, which needs a working on-screen UI ([Home Assistant community](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/is-this-the-perfect-standalone-tablet-for-ha/658422/206)). In that case keep the stock launcher enabled throughout, so the UI never disappears.

Next, take a snapshot. Without root you can't back up most app data; `adb backup` excludes app data on Android 12+ unless the app is debuggable ([Android Developers: backup-leaks](https://developer.android.com/privacy-and-security/risks/backup-leaks)). What you can capture, and what matters, is package state. CleanYourTV's `disable.sh` writes its own record to `~/.cleanyourtv/` before changing anything ([README "Restoring"](https://github.com/Gathin23/cleanyourtv#restoring-your-tv)). Add an independent snapshot of your own, because no tool yet knows what a Micromax looks like:

```bash
D=~/micromax-backup/$(date +%F); mkdir -p "$D"; cd "$D"
adb shell getprop > getprop.txt
adb shell pm list packages -f -u > pkgs_all.txt
for f in d e s 3; do adb shell pm list packages -$f > pkgs_$f.txt; done
adb shell dumpsys package > dumpsys_package.txt
adb shell cmd package resolve-activity --brief -a android.intent.action.MAIN -c android.intent.category.HOME > home.txt
for t in global secure system; do adb shell settings list $t > settings_$t.txt; done
adb shell dumpsys webviewupdate > webview.txt; adb shell wm size > wm_size.txt
```

Pull APKs with `pm path` and `adb pull` for any *updated* system app or third-party app you might remove, including every `split_config.*.apk` ([APK extractor guide](https://mintlify.com/KilzaNiko/apk-extractor-adb/guides/extracting-apks)). Factory versions stay on /system, so `install-existing` restores them without a backup. Then do the parts no command can. Photograph the picture, sound and source settings screens, because vendor TV settings live in root-only vendor databases. Photograph the rear model label. Open the back cover and photograph the **mainboard silkscreen and the panel part number**. Firmware is matched by board code + panel + RAM/eMMC, not by retail model ([elektroda](https://www.elektroda.pl/rtvforum/topic3750962.html)). Without those photos, a reflash is guesswork.

Reversibility depends on which command you use. `pm disable-user --user 0` is undone with `pm enable` and keeps app data. `pm uninstall -k --user 0` is undone with `cmd package install-existing` because the APK stays on /system. Plain `pm uninstall` without `--user 0` removes the package for all users. `pm clear` destroys data for good ([Android Developers: adb](https://developer.android.com/tools/adb); [UAD-ng FAQ](https://github.com/Universal-Debloater-Alliance/universal-android-debloater-next-generation/wiki/FAQ)). Use disable only, which is also all CleanYourTV does. Avoid UAD-ng's "Disable" button on this TV, because it also runs `clear` ([UAD-ng FAQ](https://github.com/Universal-Debloater-Alliance/universal-android-debloater-next-generation/wiki/FAQ)). If something breaks, climb down the ladder one step at a time.

| Step | Situation | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Black screen / wrong Home, ADB alive | `tools/restore.sh <list> <stock.pkg>`, or by hand: `pm enable <stock>`, `cmd package set-home-activity <stock>/<activity>`, `input keyevent HOME`; re-query with `resolve-activity`, since some builds print Success and change nothing ([restore.sh](https://github.com/Gathin23/cleanyourtv/blob/main/tools/restore.sh); [shield_optimizer PR #18](https://github.com/bryanroscoe/shield_optimizer/pull/18)) |
| 2 | ADB dead, Settings reachable (remote gear key) | Settings → Apps → show system apps → Enable the stock launcher; or re-toggle network debugging ([README Restoring](https://github.com/Gathin23/cleanyourtv#restoring-your-tv)) |
| 3 | No UI path, system boots | Safe mode to bypass the third-party launcher (Sony's method: hold power, then hold volume-down during boot animation); unverified on Micromax ([Sony support](https://www.sony.ca/en/electronics/support/televisions-projectors/articles/00127645)) |
| 4 | Boot loop or stuck logo | Recovery mode → "Wipe data/factory reset", which restores **every** user-0 change; reportedly power + volume-down on Micromax Android TVs ([tvsfaq Micromax 50K2330UHD](https://www.tvsfaq.com/en/reset/micromax-50k2330uhd); [TCL recovery](https://support.tcl.com/en_US/how-to-troubleshoot-a-tcl-google-tv-in-recovery-mode)) |
| 5 | Recovery unreachable | Board-matched USB reflash (`MstarUpgrade.bin` on FAT32 ≤16 GB, power held at plug-in, ~15 min, never cut power) or a repair shop with an eMMC programmer ([Elektroda MS08GP](https://www.elektroda.com/qa,40-ms08gp-mab2hg-firmware-guide.html); [RCA manual p.27](https://www.manualslib.com/manual/1954450/Rca-55an3d.html?page=27)) |

Two cautions apply to the bottom of this table. First, the Micromax button combo comes only from an aggregator site. Many budget TVs have a single joystick button, so find your recovery entry method *before* you need it. Look in the manual, or try it once on a healthy TV and choose "Reboot system now". Second, step 5 is where real bricks happen, and Micromax makes it unusually hard. There is no official Micromax firmware portal. The main third-party host of Micromax dumps, dipelectronicslab, now shows a placeholder ([dipelectronicslab](https://dipelectronicslab.com/micromax-43t7200mhd-software/)). The CVTE and MStar boards Micromax likely uses are shared across brands, and a wrong-panel image produces a black screen, wrong colours or a dead board ([badcaps CV338H-T50](https://www.badcaps.net/forum/troubleshooting-hardware-devices-and-electronics-theory/troubleshooting-tvs-and-video-sources/3235735-graetz-akai-cv338h-t50-bricked-after-firmware-upgrade?p=3236102); [Elektroda](https://www.elektroda.com/rtvforum/topic4178421.html)). Unofficial dumps also often carry another unit's MAC address and DRM keys, which can break Netflix even after a "successful" flash. A user-0 debloat cannot corrupt /system, so reaching step 5 from debloating alone means recovery mode itself is unreachable. That is rare, and more likely after a failed OTA than after `disable-user`. Before reflashing anything, rule out backlight and PSU faults, which look identical ([electronics-tricks](https://electronics-tricks.blogspot.com/2023/11/lcd-tv-screen-repairing-classes.html)). Micromax TV service is reachable through a third-party-listed number, 0129-488 3999, with mixed reports on how easy it is to get through ([indiacustomercare](https://www.indiacustomercare.com/micromax-tv-customer-care-number); [TelecomTalk](https://telecomtalk.info/impossible-to-reach-micromax-customer-support/118911)). Finally, OTAs undo your work: they re-enable listed packages and hand Home back to the stock launcher. `tools/check.py <list> --fix` re-applies your list after an update ([README "After a firmware update"](https://github.com/Gathin23/cleanyourtv#after-a-firmware-update)). Given how few updates this hardware gets, that is a minor cost.

## Conclusion

The main danger here is uncertainty, not the commands. Every tool involved can be undone, and a factory reset covers everything a non-root debloat can do. The real exposure on a Micromax is that you don't yet know your SoC, your stock launcher package, whether port 5555 survives a cold boot, or how to reach recovery mode. All four are knowable in an evening, before you change anything. Do the pre-flight (generation check, cold-boot ADB test, snapshot, board photos, a rehearsal of recovery mode) and the worst realistic outcome falls from "bricked TV" to "redo the setup wizard and rescan channels."

The bigger implication is architectural. Building your character UI as an ordinary app first, and promoting it to Home last, turns a risky firmware-adjacent project into a normal frontend dev loop with a safety net. It is also the only path that works on a Canvas 3, where CleanYourTV's launcher can't install. Since no Micromax list exists anywhere, the package inventory, `getprop` dump and safe-disable list you produce will be the first public record for this hardware. Contributing it to CleanYourTV's `docs/devices/` turns your experiment into the tested list the next Micromax owner currently lacks.
